Theory

Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP). Given n cities, find the shortest closed route that visits every city exactly once and returns to the start. There are (n−1)!/2 possible tours, so checking all of them is impossible for even moderate n. TSP is NP-hard, so heuristics are used to find good (not always optimal) tours.

Simulated annealing (SA). SA is inspired by annealing in metallurgy: a metal is heated and then cooled slowly so its atoms settle into a low-energy state. Here, the “energy” is the tour length and the “temperature” T controls how willing the search is to accept worse tours.

Algorithm.

  1. Start with a random tour and a high temperature T.
  2. Propose a neighbour: pick two random positions i < j and reverse the tour between them (a 2-opt swap). This removes two edges and adds two new ones.
  3. Compute Δ = new length − old length.
  4. If Δ < 0 (shorter), accept. Otherwise accept with probability p = e−Δ/T, by comparing p with a random number r in [0, 1): accept if r < p.
  5. Cool down: T ← α · T with α slightly below 1. Repeat from step 2 until T is very small.

Why it works. At high T, p is close to 1 even for bad moves, so the search explores widely and can escape local minima (the tour length graph is noisy and goes up and down). As T falls, bad moves are accepted less and less, and the search behaves like a plain downhill search that settles into a short tour. A slower cooling rate (α closer to 1) usually gives a better tour but takes more iterations.
